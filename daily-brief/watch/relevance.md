# Watch — what survives

Decision criteria for the watch domain. Written as tests an item has to pass,
not as topics, because topic lists over-fire: "MCP, agents, Claude Code"
matches half of every issue.

The tests are anchored to what stays true about Hunter, not to what he is
building this week: the guiding principle and top three priorities in
`~/workspace/second-brain/notes/longterm-priorities.md`, and the threads in
`notes/current-priorities.md` that have held for months (credibility as an
AI practitioner, local community, mindfulness). Reread this file when either
note changes, not on a schedule. The tension is real: too broad and nothing
is filtered, too specific and it echoes last week back. The line drawn here
is that a test asks about consequence for a durable goal, never about
whether a story sounds related.

## An item survives if

At least one of these is true, and none of the exclusions below apply.

1. **It changes how I would build my personal AI system.** The harness,
   agents, memory, and surfaces I run for myself, in whatever shape they
   take this year. It passes if I would adopt or reject a practice because
   of it. It fails if it is a launch, a demo, or a take, however good.
2. **It is worth raising with my local AI community.** The meetups, huddles,
   and practitioners I talk shop with in Nashville, whichever groups those
   are at the time. It passes if a working practitioner would not already
   have seen it and I can say it in two sentences.
3. **It changes the odds on my career bet.** I left a platform architect job
   to build in AI, with a decision point on founder versus role. It passes
   only if it names a concrete shift in demand for what I do, or a specific
   company, role, or person I could act on. A headline about the AI job
   market or AI startups in general fails; there is one every day.
4. **It changes a premise my investment policy rests on.** cfo's investment
   policy is the standard; watch reads it, read-only, to judge this test.
   It passes if a shift in market structure or in who captures value would
   make cfo rewrite a clause. News about a holding fails; the policy
   already says deviation is not news.
5. **It is a Nashville event or change I would act on.** It passes if it
   connects me to people, groups, or places tied to what I am about: AI
   practitioners, meditation and mindfulness, entrepreneurs, the wellness
   space and the podcast I want to build one day, family life in the city.
   It fails if it is merely local.

## Excluded

- Model launches with no API or behavior change I can use.
- Funding rounds and valuations.
- Benchmark-only results.
- Opinion pieces about AI risk with no practical consequence for what I build.
- Anything I have already seen in the last fourteen days, from any source.
- Product marketing from a vendor, even a vendor I use.

## Limits

- At most five items a week. One or two is the normal week. Zero is fine
  and is not a failure.
- Each item names its source in the headline and carries the story's link as
  its receipt.
- Dedupe by story, not by wording, across a fourteen-day window.
- Items that pass the tests but not the cap are kept, not discarded. They
  are the raw material for a feed on the dashboard, which is a later
  dashboard-lane piece, not part of the brief.

## Review

Two weeks after the first Watch section ships, the curator looks at the
Watch marks in `briefs/feedback-*.md` for those two weeks and writes one
line in the lane handoff: how many items were approved, dismissed, or left
unmarked. Mostly dismissed or unmarked means the tests are too loose;
narrow them or drop the section. Mostly approved means leave them alone.
The marks are the only measure. Nothing infers whether Hunter read a line.
